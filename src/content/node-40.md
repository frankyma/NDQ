_"In truth there is only one mind. This is the doctrine of the Upanishads."_ - Schrodinger

In 1964, John Stewart Bell devised a thought experiment to test whether entangled particles can influence each other across arbitrary distances.

Through the 1970s and '80s, John Clauser, Alain Aspect, and Anton Zeilinger carried out experiments demonstrating correlations between particles that are too strong and too fast to be explained by any signal traveling between them. The result forces a stark conclusion: the universe must be either non-local or non-real. Non-locality would mean that the universe is not bound by inherent distances, allowing influence to propagate beyond the constraints of space. Non-reality would mean that fundamental particles do not possess definite properties until the moment they are measured.
