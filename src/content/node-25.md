Meister Eckhart (c. 1260–1328) is the most celebrated Christian mystical theologian — a Dominican friar whose sermons and treatises pressed Christian language toward its limits.

His central teaching is the **Seelengrund** — the Ground of the Soul: a depth within the soul so simple and intimate that it is identical with the Ground of God.

*"The eye with which I see God is the same eye with which God sees me."*

Eckhart distinguishes two levels of the divine:

- **God** (*Gott*): the personal, relational Creator — the God of prayer, love, and revelation
- **The Godhead** (*Gottheit*): the unmanifest, undifferentiated source beyond all names, attributes, and even "existence" as we understand it. God emerges from the Godhead; the soul's deepest return is to the Godhead itself.

This maps remarkably onto the Advaita Vedanta distinction between *Saguṇa Brahman* (God with attributes) and *Nirguṇa Brahman* (the absolute beyond all attributes).

For Eckhart, the birth of the Word in the soul is not a distant event — it is the continuous arising of the divine in the ground of awareness, here, now, always already.
