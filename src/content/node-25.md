_"The eye with which I see God is the same eye with which God sees me."_ — Meister Eckhart

Meister Eckhart (c. 1260–1328) was a Dominican friar and is often called the most celebrated Christian mystical theologian. His central teaching is the _Seelengrund_ — the Ground of the Soul: a depth within the soul so simple and intimate that it is identical with the Ground of God.

Eckhart distinguishes two levels of the divine. _Gott_ (God) is the personal, relational Creator: the God of prayer, love, and revelation. _Gottheit_ (the Godhead) is the unmanifest, undifferentiated source beyond all names, attributes, and even "existence" as we usually mean it. God emerges from the Godhead; the soul's deepest return is to the Godhead itself.

This maps closely onto the Advaita Vedanta distinction between _Saguṇa Brahman_ (God with attributes) and _Nirguṇa Brahman_ (the absolute beyond all attributes).

For Eckhart, the birth of the Word in the soul is not a distant event. It is happening here and now in the ground of our being. We do not need to earn our way closer to God; we only need to recognize that, at the deepest level, we are of divinity.
