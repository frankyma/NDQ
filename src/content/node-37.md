There is seemingly a major contradiction between Buddhism and Vedanta. Vedanta, like all Hinduism, stresses the concept of the **Atman** or the eternal self, whereas a core tenet of Buddhism is **Anatman** or Not-Self.

## Buddhism

It's important to know that the Buddha did not strictly teach that there is no self. In fact, when he was directly asked, "Is there a self?" he remained silent. (The Unanswerable Questions are a famous set of questions that the Buddha remained silent to.) What Buddha taught was that the components of the body-mind that we typically latch on as "I", when examined closely, are not inherent, unchanging selves.

## Advaita Vedanta

It is true that most dualistic schools of Hinduism do believe in an eternal self, the Atman. However, Advaita (non-dual) Vedanta collapses the duality between the Atman (eternal self) and Brahman (ultimate consciousness). This means the self is not anything that might appear as an object to consciousness, same as the Buddhist proposition, nor an entity separate from anything else.

The ground of the self is equal to the ground of all existence, which is Sat-chit-ananda (Existence consciousness bliss). This concept of the ground is also found in Vajrayana Buddhism and Christian mysticism.
