_"The Father and I are one."_ — Jesus (John 10:30)

Christian mysticism seeks direct union with God, not merely belief about God. Some read Jesus himself as a non-dual teacher: not only pointing toward the Father, but speaking from oneness with Him. The tradition that follows — from medieval mystics to modern contemplatives — keeps returning to that same recognition.

One of its deepest methods is **apophatic theology**, or the _via negativa_. It holds that God finally exceeds every concept: even words like "good," "powerful," or "existing" fall short. So the mystic learns to say what God is _not_. This is not skepticism. It is a way of clearing the mind of concepts so the soul can approach what no description can hold.
