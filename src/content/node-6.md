Vedanta is a major philosophical school of Hinduism based on the end verses of the Vedas (the oldest, foundational scriptures of Hinduism). These sections are also known as the Upanishads.

Three foundational texts:

- **Upanishads**: direct inquiry into the nature of consciousness and reality
- **Brahma Sūtras**: 555 sutras written by the sage Vyasa, dealing with the knowledge of Brahman.
- **Bhagavad Gītā**: the warrior Arjuna's dialogue with Krishna on self, duty, and liberation

Key Terminology:

- **Brahman**: the supreme, ultimate reality that is the ground of all existence. Loosly maps to "God" in the Western language.
- **Ātman**: the innermost self, beneath our body-mind
- **Māyā**: the illusion of that creates this phenomenological and separate world.
- **Moksha**: liberation — the direct recognition of one's true nature
