Vedanta is the philosophical tradition rooted in the Upanishads — the final and most philosophical portion of the Vedas. "Advaita" means non-dual, and it is the most prominent branch of Vedanta.

Three foundational texts:

- **Upanishads**: direct inquiry into the nature of consciousness and reality
- **Brahma Sūtras**: a systematic framework that rejects the dualism of Sāṃkhya philosophy
- **Bhagavad Gītā**: the warrior Arjuna's dialogue with Krishna on self, duty, and liberation

## Core Teachings

- **Ātman = Brahman**: individual consciousness and universal consciousness are ultimately the same
- **Māyā**: the illusion that veils this identity, producing the appearance of separateness
- **Moksha**: liberation — the direct recognition of one's true nature
- *Tat tvam asi* — "That thou art"

## The Four Yogas

Advaita emphasizes **Jñāna Yoga** — the yoga of inquiry and discernment — above the other paths (Karma, Bhakti, Rāja).

## Analogies

- **Water and waves**: waves are real, but made entirely of water
- **Ornaments and gold**: different forms, one substance
- **Dreams**: dreamed characters appear separate, but arise from a single dreaming awareness
