## Plants and Consciousness

Plants exhibit behaviors that challenge the assumption that consciousness requires a brain.

- Roots detect moisture and grow toward it — a form of decision-making without a nervous system
- The dodder parasite detects light wavelengths to locate and move toward a host plant
- Plants respond to damage, communicate via chemical signals, and retain memory of prior stresses

If awareness or responsiveness extends this far, the boundary between "conscious" and "non-conscious" becomes less obvious than assumed.

## The Split-Brain Experiment

When the corpus callosum — the bridge between the brain's two hemispheres — is severed, something striking emerges.

- A frightening image shown only to the right hemisphere causes fear in the patient
- The language-dominant left hemisphere didn't see the image and has no explanation
- Rather than admitting ignorance, the left brain **instantly fabricates** a plausible story: "That researcher looks creepy," "The room is too dark"

The unified self is a construction. The narrator invents reasons after the fact and believes them completely.

What divides is the mind, not awareness itself. Splitting the brain is like splitting a camera lens — it fractures the image, but not the light.
