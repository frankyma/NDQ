Richard Rohr (b. 1943) is a Franciscan friar widely regarded as the most influential contemporary Christian mystic and the founder of the Center for Action and Contemplation in Albuquerque, New Mexico.

His central teaching is the **Universal Christ**: "Christ" is not simply Jesus' last name, but refers to the divine presence embedded in all of creation — the cosmic incarnation of the divine in matter, in humanity, in everything that exists.

Jesus becomes the clearest *example* of non-duality: fully human and fully divine simultaneously — not one at the expense of the other. In classical theological terms: the hypostatic union. For Rohr, this is not a unique exception but an *illustration* of the nature of reality.

The primary obstacle to recognizing this is the **False Self** — the ego-constructed identity built from roles, wounds, reputation, and achievement. Spiritual practice is essentially ego death: not the destruction of the person, but the dissolution of the illusion that the ego is the totality of who we are.

The True Self is not a better ego — it is what remains when the ego stops insisting it is the whole story.
