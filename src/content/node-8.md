_"The universe is His shadow."_ — Ibn ‘Arabi

**Sufism** is the mystical tradition within Islam—the inward path of purifying the ego, opening the heart, and directly experiencing the presence of God.

The teaching that God is one can deepen into a more radical recognition: nothing exists independently of God. Everything depends upon the one Reality for its existence.

### Core Recognition

The separate self isn’t as solid as it seems. As attachment to the ego loosens, awareness becomes a clearer mirror of the Divine. The seeker may move from seeing God beyond the world, to seeing God within everything, and finally to seeing nothing apart from God.

### Unity of Being (*Wahdat al-Wujud*)

In this influential Sufi teaching, existence is one. The cosmos is the ongoing self-disclosure of the Divine—every mountain, tree, person, and moment reveals something of God.

This is the paradox of *He / not He*: everything reflects the Divine, yet no particular thing contains or defines God. God is beyond everything and present through everything.

Other traditions approach this recognition differently. Sufism sees the world as divine disclosure, Advaita sees its substance as Brahman, and Buddhism teaches that everything is empty of independent existence. Their metaphysics differ, but each challenges the idea that anything stands alone.

### Contemplative Practice

A central Sufi practice is *dhikr*, the remembrance of God through sacred words or divine names. Repetition, sometimes joined with the breath, draws attention away from the ego and back toward the Divine.

*Muraqaba* is the quiet observation of the heart—watching thoughts and impulses while remaining aware of God’s presence. These practices lead toward *fana*, the fading of the ego’s claim to separateness, followed by *baqa*: returning to ordinary life grounded in remembrance and aligned with the Divine will.
