Sufism is the mystical and contemplative tradition within Islam, seeking direct experience of the divine rather than merely observing external law.

Central to Sufism is **fanāʾ** — the annihilation or dissolution of the ego-self in union with God. This is not destruction but a seeing-through of the false separate self. What follows fanāʾ is **baqāʾ** — subsistence in God: not absence, but a transformed, ego-free presence.

The 13th-century Persian poet Rumi remains the most widely read voice of this tradition. For Rumi, all longing is the soul's memory of its origin — the reed flute's cry for the reed bed from which it was cut.

Key themes:

- The **heart** (*qalb*) as the organ of spiritual perception, not the intellect
- **Love** (*ʿishq*) as the path — not mere belief or ritual
- The **murshid** (teacher): the guide who has made the journey and can point the way
- The **dhikr**: remembrance of God through repeated invocation, dissolving the sense of a separate rememberer
