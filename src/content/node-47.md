**Solipsism** is the view that only one's own mind is guaranteed to exist. Because you cannot step outside experience, other people and the outer world cannot be proven real. At best they are appearances; at worst, inventions.

There is a real overlap here. We have already seen that "things" do not inherently exist outside of consciousness. Every object, body, and world you have ever known has appeared *in* experience. Solipsism notices this, and stops there.

Where it goes wrong is the border it quietly keeps: **self** as verifiably real, **other** as unverifiable or false. That border is the illusion of a separate self. Solipsism is what non-dual insight looks like when it is still trapped inside a personal "I" — a private mind that owns experience and faces a doubtful world.

But the self that solipsism trusts is itself an appearance: a body, a stream of thoughts, a viewpoint. It is not the ground of experience; it appears *within* experience, just as "other people" do. Once that border is seen through, the question "are others real?" softens. In a dream, many characters appear, yet they are not proven false by being made of mind — they *are* mind, appearing as many. Waking life is the same: all is inherently made of awareness. Others are not denied; the separate owner of experience is.

Solipsism says: only my mind exists.
Non-duality says: there is only mind — and "mine" was never a wall.
