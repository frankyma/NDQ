When the corpus callosum (the bridge between the brain's two hemispheres) is severed, something striking emerges. Surgeons originally did this to treat severe epilepsy. The seizures often stopped. What Roger Sperry and Michael Gazzaniga found in the patients afterward was stranger.

A frightening image shown only to the right hemisphere causes fear in the patient. The language-dominant left hemisphere didn't see the image and has no explanation. Rather than admitting ignorance, the left brain instantly fabricates a plausible story: "That researcher looks creepy," or "The room is too dark."

The same pattern shows up elsewhere. Show an image only to the right hemisphere and the patient can't name it (speech lives mostly on the left), but the left hand can still pick out the matching object. One side knows. The other side talks, and denies knowing. Sometimes the hands even disagree: one buttoning a shirt while the other unbuttons it. Again the left hemisphere invents a confident reason for whatever just happened.

The unified self is a construction. We invents reasons after the fact and believes them completely. What divides is the mind, not awareness itself.
