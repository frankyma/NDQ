**Panpsychism** is the view that consciousness, or at least some proto-experience, is present in all fundamental physical things — electrons, quarks, spacetime points. It tries to solve the hard problem by never letting consciousness "arise" from dead matter: if matter was always a little bit conscious, the gap seems smaller.

This can sound like saying consciousness is fundamental. It is not the same claim.

Panpsychism still treats the physical world as a collection of **inherently existing units**, and then adds consciousness as a property of each unit. Non-duality has already shown that no such independent units exist. As Nagarjuna and modern physics both point out, what we call "things" only appear relative to other things — empty of inherent self-nature. There are no little subjects waiting to be painted with awareness.

This is also why panpsychism faces the **combination problem**: if each particle has its own scrap of experience, how do billions of scraps become *one* felt life — the single, unified field of your seeing, hearing, and thinking? The problem only arises if you start with many consciousnesses. Non-duality starts the other way around: there is one awareness, and the appearance of many things (and many people) arises within it — like waves in an ocean, not bricks stacked into a building.

Panpsychism tries to upgrade matter into mind. Non-duality recognizes that matter was never outside mind to begin with.
