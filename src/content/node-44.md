_Act like you have free will and do the best work you can._ — Swami Sarvapriyananda

Do we have free will?

The simple answer is: no — at least not in the sense we typically believe.

Our intuition is that there's an **I** that makes its own decisions.

However, at the ultimate level, the self is an illusion, so there's no inherent I to possess that will in the first place.

Studies bear this out: the mind makes decisions well before they reach conscious awareness. In a 2008 study, neuroscientist John-Dylan Haynes placed participants in a brain scanner and asked them to press a button with either their left or right hand whenever they felt the urge. By analyzing the scans, the researchers could predict which hand a participant would choose several seconds before the person was consciously aware of having decided — the "will" is often observed, not authored.

Try this experiment (credits to Sam Harris): Close your eyes, count three seconds, and think of the first movie that comes to mind. (This works much better with auditory instructions, so if you already had a movie in mind going in, wipe it and reach for the next one.) Notice how the movie simply appears out of the space of consciousness. Where is the "will" in that? And where is the entity that takes ownership of the choice?

It's easy to feel this conclusion to be pessimistic. Far from it — letting go of a traditional free will offers a great deal of freedom.

For one, it lets us hold far more compassion for others. We see that all of us act out of the causes and conditions of our past. Furthermore, ur minds still retain agency: the agency to recognize how its been shaped by that past, and to use the tools at its disposal to shape a better future.
