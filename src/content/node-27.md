In 1972, a plane crashed in the Andes near Uruguay at 12,000 ft. Many people died or were seriously injured. The conditions in the mountains were extremely harsh: the air was thin and bitterly cold, nighttime temperatures plunged well below freezing, and frequent blizzards buried the wreckage in snow. They had almost no food, were dressed for a summer afternoon rather than a high-alpine winter, and were surrounded on all sides by towering peaks that cut off any connection to civilization. After 10 days of torment, the survivors heard on the radio that the government had given up the search.

Arturo, one of the survivors, suffered major injuries in the crash, and over the next few weeks, got weaker and weaker.

One of the boys asked him, "What good is God in this environment?"

“You are angry at the God you were taught to believe in as a child,” Arturo answered. “The God who is supposed to watch over you and protect you, who answers your prayers and forgives your sins. This God is just a story. Religions try to capture God, but God is beyond religion. The true God lies beyond our comprehension. We can’t understand His will; He can’t be explained in a book. He didn’t abandon us and He will not save us. He has nothing to do with our being here. God does not change, He simply is. I don’t pray to God for forgiveness or favors, I only pray to be closer to Him, and when I pray, I fill my heart with love. When I pray this way, I know that God is love. When I feel that love, I remember that we don’t need angels or a heaven, because we are a part of God already.”

A few days before his death, Arturo was essentially immobile.

“I know I am getting closer to God,” he said. “Sometimes I feel His presence so close to me. I can feel His love, Nando. There’s so much love, I want to cry.”

One night, Arturo started to cry softly. When his friend asked him why, he responded, “Because I am so close to God.” He died a few days later.

Parrado, Nando, and Vince Rause. *Miracle in the Andes: 72 Days on the Mountain and My Long Trek Home* (p. 93).
