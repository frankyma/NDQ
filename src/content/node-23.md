Rinzai Zen, brought to Japan by Eisai (1141–1215), is distinguished by its use of **koans** — paradoxical questions or statements given by a teacher to a student as objects of meditation.

Classic examples: *"What is the sound of one hand clapping?"* *"What was your face before your parents were born?"*

A koan cannot be resolved by conceptual thinking. That is precisely the point. It is designed to exhaust the discursive mind — to push the student to the edge of thought, and through that edge. The resolution is not a clever answer but a direct recognition: a sudden breakthrough (*kensho* or *satori*) that cannot be arrived at by reasoning.

Non-duality cannot be stated without immediately becoming a concept — and a concept of non-duality is still duality. The koan forces contact with what cannot be conceptualized. It is a finger pointing at the moon; the teacher watches to see if the student looks at the finger or the moon.
