Soto Zen was brought from China to Japan by the monk Dogen (1200–1253), who articulated its central practice with unusual precision and depth.

The practice is **shikantaza** — "just sitting." Not meditation *in order to* achieve enlightenment at some future point, but sitting that *is* the expression of enlightenment here and now. The means and the goal are not separate.

*"To study the self is to forget the self. To forget the self is to be enlightened by all things."* — Dogen

This is non-duality expressed as method. The practitioner does not try to transcend the present moment or arrive somewhere else — they enter it completely, without agenda, without grasping. In the absence of the reaching mind, what remains is unobstructed.

Practice and realization are one. There is no path leading to a destination that is not already the ground you are standing on.
